Permits & Provo Zoning

Most ADU permits in Utah County are processed through the Provo building and planning department. We handle intake, plan review coordination and inspection scheduling for you from start to finish, so you never have to deal with the city directly.

What's different about ADUs in Provo. Provo's ADU market is unusually driven by BYU's student-rental demand, and the city is still finalizing its SB284 compliance ordinance. A draft proposal would cap all accessory structures at 40% of total parcel area, with published maps showing which neighborhoods gain new eligibility.

Utah's New ADU Law (Effective October 1, 2026)

Utah's SB284 now requires most cities to allow one detached ADU per qualifying lot statewide. Provo's east-bench neighborhoods generally have larger lots that clear the 11,000-sq-ft threshold; older, smaller downtown-adjacent lots near campus are more likely to need an internal conversion. THE PROVO DETAILS

Provo's ADU Code Was Built to Stop Student Rentals — Not to Enable Them

Provo's accessory apartment ordinance predates SB284 by years, and it reads that way: owner-occupancy is required, occupancy is capped, and a rental license is mandatory. If your goal is extended family or rental income from an owner-occupied home, this works in your favor — but the rules are built to prevent a pure student-rental play.

Provo Runs on Five Official Districts — And a Different Feel Block to Block

As Utah County's largest city and home to BYU, Provo is organized into five official Neighborhood Districts (North, East, West, Northwest and Central) under the city's Neighborhood District Program — a formal structure most Utah County cities this size don't have. Inside those districts, several long-established named areas shape how an ADU project actually looks:

Joaquin

A named neighborhood with its own city-adopted Joaquin Neighborhood Plan, closer to downtown and BYU.

Franklin

An established residential area within Provo's district system.

Rock Canyon

East-bench homes near the Rock Canyon trailhead and foothills, part of Provo's east side.

Grandview

Split into North and South areas, a long-established residential part of the city.

Foothill

Higher-elevation homes on Provo's eastern edge, with the terrain considerations that come with bench and foothill lots.

Because Provo has such a mix of century-old in-fill lots, BYU-adjacent rental-heavy blocks and newer bench subdivisions, we confirm your zone and district before pricing anything — a Joaquin in-fill lot and a Foothill bench lot can call for genuinely different ADU approaches.

Provo's Accessory Apartment Code Is Built Around Owner-Occupancy

Provo City Code Title 14.30 governs accessory apartments, and it's noticeably stricter on occupancy than a lot of Utah County ordinances, which makes sense in a city with a huge student rental market to manage:

RequirementProvo Standard
Owner-occupancyRequired for the primary one-family dwelling
Occupancy capMaximum 3 unrelated adults
Minimum detached ADU size200 sq ft
Detached ADU height/footprintMust stay below the primary dwelling
Parking4 spaces required (up to 2 may be tandem)
UtilitiesSeparate meter required for a detached unit

A rental dwelling license is also required, and ADUs are prohibited outright in several of Provo's higher-density zones (including PRO, R2PD, RM and the R16–R19 and R110 zones) — the zones where BYU-area student housing is concentrated. They're generally allowed in residential zones west of I-15, with some SDP-5/R2PD exceptions. We confirm your specific zone, and the exact permit fee from the city's current fee schedule, before pricing your project.

Provo Hasn't Finalized Its SB284 Ordinance Yet

Unlike some Utah County cities that already had a detached-ADU rule in place, Provo was still working through its response to Utah's statewide SB284 mandate as of early September 2026. City staff proposed a 40%-of-parcel accessory-structure limit for detached ADUs, but as of the most recent reporting, that proposal hadn't yet gone through Planning Commission or a council vote — with the state's October 1, 2026 compliance deadline just weeks away. Because this is actively moving, we check the current status with Provo's Community Development Department at the time we scope your project rather than quoting a rule that may have changed.

What Does an ADU Cost in Provo?

Provo's occupancy caps and rental-license requirement mean most projects here are sized for a genuine second household rather than maximum rentable bedrooms, which keeps scope more in line with national averages rather than a maxed-out student-rental build-out.
