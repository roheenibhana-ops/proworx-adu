What Actually Triggers a Building Permit for an ADU in Draper?

Not every part of an ADU conversion needs a separate permit review, but most of the ones that matter do:

New footings, framing or any change to the structure's footprint
New or relocated plumbing and electrical circuits
Basement egress windows for any bedroom
Separate utility metering, where the ADU is metered independently

What Gets Inspected During an ADU Build in Draper, and When?

Every ADU we build in Draper goes through the same three inspection stages: framing (before drywall goes up), rough electrical and plumbing (before walls close), and a final inspection before move-in. Egress windows get inspected specifically for size and clearance — a common point where unpermitted basement conversions fail if a homeowner tries to sell or refinance later.

Do I Need HOA Approval in Addition to a Draper City Permit?

If your property is in one of Draper's HOA-governed communities like Suncrest, clearing your city permit doesn't automatically clear your HOA's architectural review — they're separate approvals, and homeowners sometimes assume one covers the other. Most HOA design boards in these communities focus on exterior massing, materials and placement more than interior layout, but they still need to sign off before you build.

We submit both in parallel from the start of your project, which is usually where an ADU either saves a few weeks or loses them.

What Should a Real Draper ADU Quote Include?

A quote that doesn't account for Draper's specific conditions isn't necessarily wrong on purpose — it's usually just based on a generic countywide template. Before you sign anything, make sure your quote itemizes:

Soils or engineering review, if your lot needs one
HOA submission time priced into the schedule, not just build time
Permit fees itemized by category, not bundled into a lump sum
Utility separation costs, if you're metering the ADU independently
