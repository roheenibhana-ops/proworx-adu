What Does Utah's New ADU Law (SB284) Mean for West Jordan Homeowners?

Utah's SB284 now requires most cities to allow one detached ADU per qualifying lot statewide. Many of West Jordan's older, larger-lot neighborhoods clear the SB284 threshold comfortably. Newer, tighter subdivisions are more likely to need an internal ADU. See the full breakdown on our 2026 ADU law page →.

Internal vs. Detached: Two Very Different Bars to Clear

West Jordan's ADU ordinance (adopted under Ordinance No. 21-18, years ahead of SB284) treats internal and detached units very differently:

RequirementWest Jordan Standard
Internal ADUAllowed in all R-1 zones, no stated minimum lot size
Detached ADUOnly on lots 10,000 sq ft or larger, and only in PC, LSFR, and VLSFR zones
Detached ADU sizeSmaller than the main house, capped at 20% of combined rear and side yard area
Setbacks (detached)6 ft from the primary dwelling, 15 ft rear, 8 ft interior side, 20 ft corner side
Parking1 additional paved, non-tandem space, 9′×18′ minimum

A detached ADU also isn't allowed on a lot with a failing septic system, and it can't be paired with multi-family, mobile-home or attached/townhome-style housing. Because the detached path only works in specific zones, we confirm your exact zone designation with West Jordan's Community Development Department before we tell you what's possible.

West Jordan Cares About a Rental License, Not Who Lives Where

Unlike most Utah cities we build in, West Jordan's ordinance doesn't hinge on an owner-occupancy requirement. Instead, the trigger is simpler: if either the main house or the ADU is rented out, you need a valid business license for that rental. That's a meaningfully different compliance path than a city like Provo or Highland, where owner-occupancy of the main home is the rule itself.
